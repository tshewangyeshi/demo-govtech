## Problem Statement

JDWNRH (Jigme Dorji Wangchuck National Referral Hospital), Bhutan's national referral hospital, has no online appointment system. Patients get a physical queue token and wait 4-6 hours in person to see a doctor.

## Demand Evidence

- Founder personally witnessed the 4-6 hour wait, corroborated by others: "I witnessed personally and also heard people say."
- Both patients and family members physically wait: "The patient as well as the family is standing and waiting."
- Founder is the target user: as a working civil servant, he cannot afford to spend a full day waiting for a token, and wants an online scheduled appointment time instead.

## Status Quo

The real "workaround" isn't a formal alternative — it's an informal favor economy. People with personal connections at the hospital skip the queue entirely and go directly to the doctor's chamber: "The workaround cost them their precious time. And people who know some people in the hospital they dont need to wait and they directly get into the doctors chamber." Everyone without a connection eats the full 4-6 hour wait.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

Primary target (validated): the founder himself — a civil servant with a smartphone who needs to reserve a specific time instead of standing in a physical queue all day.

Wedge caveat (open, see below): whether "tech-comfortable working adults" are actually a large enough slice of JDWNRH's real outpatient volume is unverified — JDWNRH is a *national referral* hospital, so its patient mix may skew toward referred/rural patients rather than urban office workers. This needs to be checked before committing engineering resources to that wedge exclusively.

## Constraints

- No existing digital appointment infrastructure at JDWNRH to build on top of.
- Real patient load mixes walk-ins, referrals, and emergencies with any online-booked patients — a pure calendar-booking tool would not reflect how the hospital actually runs.
- A confirmed-slot system requires JDWNRH's institutional cooperation or data access; without it, only a request/queue-position tool is buildable.
- Concurrent-write reconciliation is a first-class technical risk, not a detail to defer: if Approach B integrates with JDWNRH's own scheduling system, the walk-in desk, the referral desk, and the online booking layer can all write to the same capacity at once. This needs race-condition-safe slot handling (e.g., `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE`-style locking) regardless of which approach ships — it applies more, not less, once multiple channels share one queue.
- Patient data handling requirements are undefined pending JDWNRH / Ministry of Health data-sharing terms. Any stored patient contact/appointment data needs a privacy and security posture agreed with the hospital before real patient data flows through the system — not assumed as a later detail.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Request & Queue-Position Tool (minimal viable)
Mobile-web app for one pilot department: patient requests a time window, sees live queue position, gets notified near their turn. A human admin manually confirms/rejects requests against a hand-set daily capacity — no real hospital data feed. Effort S, Risk Low. Ships fast, makes queue position visible/auditable (attacks the connections-bypass problem directly), but is a stopgap, not a true booking system, and still rests on the unverified wedge assumption.

### Approach B: Confirmed-Slot Booking Platform (ideal architecture) — CHOSEN (blocked, see Dependencies)
Full appointment system integrated with JDWNRH's actual scheduling data, handling both booked slots and walk-in/referral queues in one system, with an admin dashboard for hospital staff. Effort/Risk labels (XL / High) are directional only, not scoped estimates — real scoping isn't possible until JDWNRH's actual data-feed shape and capacity constraints are known (see Assignment). Solves the real, full-scale problem and properly handles the mixed queue (premise 2), but is fully blocked on institutional buy-in before a single confirmed slot can work, and has a long timeline with no early usage signal.

**"Confirmed slot" definition:** a patient receives a specific doctor + time window that the hospital's own system also recognizes as occupied — not just a queue-position estimate. Given Constraints above (walk-ins, referrals, and emergencies don't fit a pure calendar), this means the system must reserve *capacity* within a department's daily throughput, not a literal fixed appointment time — e.g., "you have slot 6 of 40 in Dr. X's Tuesday queue," confirmed against the department's real daily capacity, with walk-ins/referrals drawing from the same pool. Patient-facing UI needs both an estimated clock-time window and a queue number — the number is what's actually confirmed against capacity, the time is a derived estimate that can slip if the queue runs long. This directly requires the concurrency-safety work called out in Constraints.

**Approach B scope — gated:** Do not finalize data model, API surface, or admin dashboard scope for Approach B until the Assignment (below) resolves what JDWNRH will actually grant access to. Locking implementation detail now risks building against imagined requirements that the real data feed won't match. Once access is confirmed, scope needs: the capacity/slot data model, how walk-in/referral/booked patients share one queue, identity/auth for patients (Bhutan's national Citizen ID (CID) system is a plausible fit for a civic service like this, but unconfirmed — phone-OTP is a fallback if CID integration isn't available), and what the admin dashboard must do on day one.

**Emergency preemption (currently an open question, not a confirmed requirement):** whether hospital staff can override or preempt a booked slot for an emergency walk-in is unresolved — it could turn out the answer is "no preemption allowed, emergencies use a separate track." Until JDWNRH clarifies this, treat it as unscoped rather than assumed-required.

**Edge cases to design for once scope is unblocked:** no-shows (does a missed slot free capacity immediately or after a grace window?), cancellations/rebooking, a patient with no smartphone or reliable data access (relevant given the unresolved rural/referred patient-mix question — likely needs an assisted-booking fallback via hospital staff or a kiosk, not just a mobile app), multiple appointments per patient, and Dzongkha-language support alongside English (this is a Bhutan civic service — language access isn't optional for a rural/referred patient population).

### Approach C: Community Queue-Reporting Tool (creative/lateral, rejected for now)
Crowd-sourced live wait-time/queue reporting tool, no booking, no hospital cooperation required. Would have generated real data on JDWNRH's actual patient mix (resolving the premise-3 question) and tested engagement cheaply, at the cost of not solving the actual waiting problem. Rejected in favor of going straight for the full platform.

## Recommended Approach

Approach A was recommended (fastest real-world test of demand, no hospital dependency to start). The founder chose Approach B instead — the full confirmed-slot platform. This is a legitimate, more ambitious choice, but it means the project's critical path is not code — it's institutional buy-in from JDWNRH (see Premise 4 and The Assignment below). No engineering scope reduces that dependency.

**This makes the real plan two-phase, not singular:** Phase 1 (now) is the Assignment — get JDWNRH/MoH engagement. Phase 2 is conditional: build Approach B if access is granted, or fall back to Approach A (see Dependencies) if it isn't within a reasonable window. Until Phase 1 resolves, Approach A is arguably the more likely near-term deliverable, not a footnote to B.

## Open Questions

- **(Premise-0-level, ask first)** Does JDWNRH enforce any consistent queue order today — digital or manual — or is patient order effectively unstructured/discretionary? This is more basic than whether a *digital* system of record exists (next bullet), and determines whether Approach B's unified-queue architecture is even the right shape.
- Is JDWNRH's actual OPD patient mix (urban/civil-servant walk-ins vs. referred/rural patients) enough to justify the working-adult wedge, or does the "connections bypass" problem primarily affect a different patient population? (Premise 3, revised/open.)
- Will JDWNRH (or Bhutan's Ministry of Health digital health / e-governance team) grant a pilot department and a real capacity/scheduling data feed? (Premise 4 — this gates the entire Approach B build.)
- What is the realistic path to institutional approval — a specific hospital administrator, a government digital health initiative, or a formal proposal process?
- Does JDWNRH have *any* existing digital system of record for daily patient capacity or queue state today, or would this online booking layer be the first digital system at the hospital? This changes the concurrency design materially — if the hospital side is still paper/manual, "concurrent-write reconciliation" (Constraints) means reconciling against manual staff entry, not a live database feed.
- (Phase 3 prep, flagged by /plan-ceo-review) Referral pre-booking introduces a second institutional data feed (district hospitals) writing into the same JDWNRH capacity pool as walk-ins and local online bookings. A district hospital pre-booking a slot and a local patient booking the same slot before the two feeds sync is a cross-institution double-booking, not just a within-JDWNRH race condition. Not solvable until Phase 3 is real, but worth not losing before that planning starts.

## Success Criteria

**If Phase 2 is Approach B (institutional access granted):**
- JDWNRH (or a relevant Ministry of Health body) agrees to a pilot: at least one department, a real capacity number or scheduling data access.
- Once piloted: measurable reduction in average wait time for online-booked patients in the pilot department, and visible/auditable queue position that reduces the connections-based bypass advantage.
- Connections-bypass metric (ties directly to Premise 1's thesis): track the share of patients seen who skipped the queue via personal connection, before vs. after the pilot. If this doesn't move, the product hasn't touched the real problem even if booking volume looks healthy.
- **Validity caveat (flagged by /plan-ceo-review's outside voice):** at single-department pilot scale, this metric will be noisy — small sample size plus a likely Hawthorne effect (staff behave differently once they know bypass is being measured) means a single pilot window's bypass-rate number is a directional signal, not proof the product works. Don't present pilot-stage results to JDWNRH or investors as validated impact; frame them as "early signal, needs scale to confirm."

**If Phase 2 falls back to Approach A (institutional access not obtained in time):**
- A working request-and-queue-position tool is live for at least one informal use case (e.g., the founder and people he can directly reach), with real people submitting requests and getting notified — not just a demo.
- Self-reported reduction in time spent waiting, even without hospital-side data, as a proxy for the same connections-bypass thesis.

## Design Decisions (from /plan-design-review)

A rough HTML wireframe of the wait-time page caught a real bug live: the Dzongkha toggle label rendered as broken/tofu characters — no Tibetan/Uchen script font was installed on the rendering system. This is the font-rendering dependency above, now empirically confirmed, not just a theoretical risk.

**Information architecture:**
- Wait-time page is multi-department (a list, e.g. General Medicine, Pediatrics, ENT), not scoped to a single department — matches the public-transparency goal and serves patients outside the founder's own OPD use case.
- Default language is English, with Dzongkha available via a persistent toggle (remembers the user's choice per device). Deliberately chosen over defaulting to Dzongkha, given the founder is the first real user and initial testing context.
- Typography: **Uchen** (Google Fonts) for Dzongkha — a free webfont built specifically for Dzongkha by Bhutan's own Dzongkha Development Commission, not a generic Tibetan font — paired with **IBM Plex Sans** for Latin/English text. Avoids shipping with system-ui/-apple-system as the primary font (a known "gave up on typography" signal).

**Interaction states:**

| Feature | Loading | Empty | Error | Success | Partial/Low-confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wait-time estimate | Skeleton/spinner on department list | Explicit "No recent reports — be the first to report" card with CTA, not a decayed near-zero number | Offline/connection-failure state with retry, plus a "call this department" fallback link | N/A (read-only view) | Show freshness ("updated N min ago") AND a separate confidence indicator ("based on N reports") — these are different signals and must not be conflated |
| Wait-time report submission | Button shows submitting state | N/A | Rate-limited: explicit "You've already reported recently — thanks!" message, never a silent drop | Visible confirmation the report was received | N/A |
| Caregiver-link request | "Waiting for approval" state, clearly time-boxed | N/A | OTP failure/expiry: clear retry instructions | Link confirmed, caregiver gains scoped access | "Provisional" status if the patient hasn't responded within the grace window (see below) — clearly marked as provisional, not silently treated as approved |
| Bilingual toggle | N/A | N/A | Missing translation key: falls back to English text, never a raw key or blank string | N/A | N/A |

**Caregiver-link approval fallback (critical gap, now resolved):** a patient who cannot self-approve (sedated, elderly, no signal, doesn't check their phone) gets a time-boxed grace link — the caregiver can link provisionally if the patient hasn't responded within a short window (e.g. 24h), clearly marked "provisional" in the UI on both sides, with the patient able to revoke it anytime once they're able to. This is a deliberate usability/security tradeoff, not an oversight — document it as such so a future reviewer doesn't mistake the grace window for a bug.

**Wait-time page next action:** includes a "call this department" link/number so an anxious user has somewhere to go besides just a number — no new backend or JDWNRH dependency required.

**Data model:** caregiver-patient linking is many-to-many (a patient can have multiple approved caregivers; a caregiver can be linked to multiple patients) — matches real family structures directly and is cheap to build correctly now vs. an expensive migration later.

**Accessibility (full spec, not deferred):** 44px minimum touch targets, screen-reader labels/ARIA landmarks on all interactive elements, full keyboard navigation (especially for OTP entry), and status indicators that never rely on color alone (e.g. the freshness/confidence indicators use text, not just a colored dot) — matches the user base's real demographics (elderly, possibly vision/motor-impaired patients and stressed caregivers).

**Design system:** no DESIGN.md exists yet — proceeding with universal design principles for this slice. Recommend `/design-consultation` separately once more of the product exists, to establish a durable design system rather than deriving one piecemeal per feature.
