## Baseline (Phase 1 / Approach B, from the design doc)

Source: `docs/designs/jdwnrh-hospital-booking.md` (from /office-hours, Status: APPROVED). This CEO plan is a scope-expansion layer on top of that document, not a replacement for it — read it for full premises, edge cases, and constraints.

- **Phase 1 (current, blocking everything else):** institutional buy-in from JDWNRH (or Bhutan's Ministry of Health) — a pilot department and a real capacity/data feed. No engineering scope below is buildable at production quality until this resolves.
- **Approach B (chosen, gated):** a confirmed-slot booking platform unifying walk-in, referral, and booked-slot queues into one system, with admin dashboard and concurrency-safe slot handling.
- **Premise 1 (gloss, REVISED/open per outside-voice review):** the real problem isn't "no digital booking exists" — it's that personal hospital connections let some patients skip the queue entirely while everyone else eats a 4-6 hour wait. The product's job is to make that bypass advantage obsolete, not just digitize the token counter. Previously marked AGREED with high confidence; now flagged unverified — it rests on the same single-source evidence (founder's own experience) as Premise 3, which already carries this caveat.
- **Premise 0 (new, more fundamental than any numbered premise, unresolved):** does JDWNRH enforce any consistent queue order today at all, digital or manual? If not, Approach B's unify-the-queues architecture has no authoritative queue state to integrate against — a harder problem than "missing data feed."
- **Premise 3 (gloss, revised/open):** whether the "tech-comfortable civil servant" wedge is actually a large share of JDWNRH's real patient volume is unverified — JDWNRH is Bhutan's *national referral* hospital, so referred/rural patients may be the real majority. Not yet resolved.
- **Effort scale used below:** S = single-digit days, M = 1-3 weeks, L = 3+ weeks / needs a second external party, all at solo-founder-with-AI-assistance pace, not a funded team's pace. These are directional, not committed estimates — real scoping is blocked on Phase 1 per the design doc.

### Platform Potential (cross-domain, not in scope, worth remembering)
The connections-bypass-auditability mechanism this plan builds isn't hospital-specific in principle — it's a generalizable "queue fairness engine" pattern applicable to any government service with an informal favor-economy queue problem (immigration, land registry, etc.). This is a *different* expansion axis than the healthcare-only 12-month dream state above. Not pursued now; noted here so it isn't lost.

### Build vs. Buy Consideration (raised, NOT fully resolved)
Considered integrating a commercial queue platform (WaitWell/Qmatic-style) for walk-in/booked/priority sequencing instead of custom-building it, to avoid reinventing solved queue mechanics. Founder chose to proceed with the custom build (Approach B). **Flag: the specific reasoning wasn't captured verbatim in this session** — treat this as a decision made, not a decision fully justified in writing yet. Before this is treated as settled, reconstruct the actual reasoning (likely candidates: cost of commercial licensing for a government pilot, Bhutan-specific localization/CID support not available off-the-shelf, or control over the bypass-detection logic that no commercial platform builds) and record it here or in the design doc. Revisit once JDWNRH access is confirmed and real integration constraints are known.

**Re-assessment trigger:** items 2, 4, and 6 are gated on external dependencies with no committed timeline. If Phase 1 (JDWNRH institutional access) hasn't resolved within a few months of the Assignment conversation starting, re-assess whether these gated items are still worth holding as "accepted scope" versus moving to Deferred/TODOS.md — an indefinitely gated item cluttering "accepted scope" understates how blocked the plan actually is.

**Named risk for item 2 (fairness dashboard):** two distinct risks, not one. (a) **Political/reputational** — the dashboard would surface that hospital staff extend informal priority access, which is a claim about staff behavior, not just a UX feature; expect resistance from anyone benefiting from the current system. (b) **Technical** — "bypass" needs an actual algorithmic definition before this is buildable: a patient seen out of queue order might be a connections bypass, or might be legitimate triage/emergency priority. Distinguishing the two is a nontrivial data/logic problem, likely harder than the dashboard UI itself, and is not yet designed. Treat "define and validate the bypass-detection logic with hospital input" as its own work item before the dashboard is built, not a subtask of it.

**Sequencing note (addresses the 100%-acceptance flag):** all 6 items were accepted, but they are NOT all buildable now — 3 (items 1, 3, 5) have no gate and can start immediately; 3 (items 2, 4, 6) are explicitly gated behind Phase 1 institutional access, and item 4 additionally requires a second, not-yet-attempted institutional relationship (district hospitals). Stacking three unconfirmed external dependencies (JDWNRH, district hospitals, telecom vendor) into one plan is real execution risk — track items 2, 4, and 6 as conditional scope, not committed scope, until their gates clear.
